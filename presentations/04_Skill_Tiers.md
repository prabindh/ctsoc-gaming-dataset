# Player Skill Tiers
## Estimating Expertise through Biometric Performance
**Methodology:** Using Kills Per Minute (KPM) to proxy skill levels across 51 subjects.

---

# 1. Defining the Tiers

Without explicit player ranks, we categorized subjects by their aggression and impact (KPM):
- **Pro** (Top 15%): >5.2 KPM
- **Veteran** (Next 35%): 4.7 - 5.2 KPM
- **Casual** (Next 35%): 3.8 - 4.7 KPM
- **New Player** (Bottom 15%): <3.8 KPM

---

# 2. Heart Rate Scales with Skill

Physical arousal directly correlates with skill level:
- **Pro:** 70.7 BPM
- **Veteran:** 69.6 BPM
- **Casual:** 68.7 BPM
- **New Player:** 69.1 BPM

**Why?** Pro players play a much faster, more aggressive game. Processing information and acting at high speeds requires higher cardiac output, even if they don't *feel* stressed.

---

# 3. The Stress "Bell Curve"

Unlike heart rate, psychological stress (facial expression) does not scale linearly:
- **Casuals** show the highest facial stress (Avg 18.1). They understand the game but lack total mastery, leading to frustration.
- **New Players** show the lowest facial stress (Avg 14.4). They play passively and avoid intense encounters.
- **Pros** drop to a moderate stress level (Avg 17.0). Mastery allows them to remain physically aggressive but facially composed.

---

# 4. Fear vs. Anger: The Emotion of a Kill

How players react to getting a kill reveals their experience:
- **New Players & Casuals** show significantly more **Fear** and **Neutrality** when getting a kill. For them, firefights are chaotic survival situations.
- **Pros & Veterans** show significantly more **Anger** and **Surprise**. They expect to win, and their aggressive focus is interpreted as "Anger" by the facial model.

# 6. Conclusion

- **Physiology (BPM):** Scales linearly with skill/aggression.
- **Psychology (Face):** Follows a bell curve, peaking in the "Casual" tier where players know enough to be frustrated but lack the mastery to stay calm.
